Same Start, Different Roads

Illustrative data, built to demonstrate scrolly's choreography for particle-flow stories — the cohort, neighborhoods, and outcomes below are invented for this demo, not drawn from any real study. The shape of the gap mirrors patterns documented in actual economic-mobility research.

scroll ↓

Two hundred boys, one starting line.

Picture two hundred boys, all born the same year to families earning roughly the same income. On paper their prospects look identical: the same starting resources, the same economy, the same decade to grow up in.

Where they grow up already splits them.

By age ten, geography has pulled them apart. A sizable share of these boys spent their childhoods in neighborhoods with concentrated poverty — fewer nearby jobs, thinner social networks, under-resourced schools — while the rest grew up somewhere with far more built-in opportunity.

Race compounds the neighborhood gap.

Split the same cohort by race and the imbalance sharpens: in this illustrative sample, Black boys were far more likely than white boys to have spent childhood in a high-poverty neighborhood, even though every boy here started with comparable family income.

Adulthood: who's working.

Follow them into their thirties and ask a simple question — are they employed full-time? Boys who grew up in higher-poverty neighborhoods reach adulthood with lower employment rates across the board, and the gap by race persists even inside the same neighborhood tier.

Where they land economically.

Sort the same men by adult household income relative to where they started: did they climb, hold steady, or fall behind? A larger share fell behind than climbed — and it happened even to boys whose starting numbers looked no different from their peers.

The reach, made visible.

Line the two racial groups up side by side and stack each by outcome. Despite an identical starting income, Black boys in this illustrative cohort end up crowded into the "fell behind" band far more often than white boys who started at the exact same rung — the reach of that gap, not any difference in effort or starting resources.

Every regrouping above is a full redraw driven by story.on('stepenter', …) — scroll up, jump around, the dots always land in the same place for the same step.
