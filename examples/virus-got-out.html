How the Virus Got Out

The most ambitious scrollytelling piece of its era — the New York Times' March 2020 reconstruction of the coronavirus leaving Wuhan — rebuilt as choreography on moviola. Camera flights, particle flows, the lockdown, the worldwide bloom: every scroll behavior below is CSS reacting to moviola's state machine. The map is stylized and the flows illustrative; the artwork is a stand-in, the choreography is the test.

A cluster of pneumonia cases

In December 2019, doctors in Wuhan began seeing patients with an unexplained pneumonia. The city — a metropolis of eleven million and the rail crossroads of central China — kept its ordinary rhythm while the first infections quietly multiplied.

The city keeps moving

For weeks there were no restrictions of any kind. Commuters looped through markets, offices and train stations; each circuit gave the virus another chance to jump. By the time the first alarm was raised, transmission inside the city was already self-sustaining.

Then everyone travels at once

The outbreak collided with the Lunar New Year rush — the largest annual human migration on Earth. High-speed rail carried travellers out of Wuhan to Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chengdu and beyond, and the infection travelled in the carriages with them.

The hubs light up

Flights did the same job at longer range. Seoul, Tokyo, Bangkok, Singapore: each connection seeded a new cluster days before anyone there knew to look for one. Screening at airports caught almost nothing — most travellers had no symptoms yet.

The cordon closes — too late

On January 23 China sealed Wuhan: rail, road and air, all shut. It was the largest quarantine in history, and it worked — inside the ring. But an estimated several million people had already left, and the cities they reached were incubating outbreaks of their own.

Already everywhere

Through February the map quietly filled in. Milan, Paris, London, New York, Seattle, Sydney — long-haul routes delivered the virus to every continent except Antarctica while total travel bans chased a spread that had already happened.

The bloom

Then the case counts stopped being a foreign-news story. Watch the dots as you scroll: every community outbreak traces back, hop by hop, through a train station or an airport, to that first cluster on the Yangtze.

How it got out

By March 22, 2020, the question was no longer whether the virus would arrive but how hard it would hit. It got out the way people get out: on schedules, through hubs, along the busiest routes we built — faster than any border could close.

Every behavior above — eight camera flights, flow trails drawing in and dimming under lockdown, the scroll-linked case bloom, the date HUD, the progress rail, per-chapter theming — is CSS reacting to data-camera/data-focus, data-active-step, is-shown and --story-progress.
