What's Warming the World?

Observed global temperature is the sum of several forces pulling in different directions — some pushing warming, some pulling back against it. Scroll to add each one to the chart, in roughly the order climate scientists rank their influence.

Every curve below is illustrative, hand-drawn to be plausible rather than sourced from a dataset — a demonstration of choreography, not a citation.

Factor 1 of 6

The biggest lever: greenhouse gases

Carbon dioxide and other long-lived greenhouse gases trap outgoing infrared radiation, and their atmospheric concentration has climbed steadily since industrialization, producing a warming contribution that dwarfs every other factor on this chart by the twenty-first century.

Factor 2 of 6

A modest assist from the sun

Solar output rose gently through the early twentieth century before leveling off, adding a small and largely finished warming nudge that cannot explain the acceleration observed after nineteen fifty, when solar brightness stopped climbing.

Factor 3 of 6

Volcanoes push back, briefly

Large eruptions inject reflective sulfate aerosols into the stratosphere, producing sharp temporary cooling spikes that fade within a few years once the particles settle out, visible here as short-lived dips rather than a lasting trend.

Factor 4 of 6

Cleared land, changed reflectivity

Converting forests to farmland and cities gradually altered how much sunlight the surface reflects back to space, contributing a slow, quietly negative drag that is small next to greenhouse gases but persistent across a century and a half.

Factor 5 of 6

The ocean breathes warmth in and out

Natural cycles like El Niño and La Niña shuffle heat between the ocean and the atmosphere on a roughly decade-long rhythm, creating the wobble layered on top of the steadier trends without changing the underlying long-run direction.

Factor 6 of 6

Pollution masked some of the warming

Sulfate pollution from factories and power plants scattered sunlight and cooled the planet through the mid-twentieth century, then clean-air laws scrubbed much of it away after the nineteen seventies, unmasking more of the greenhouse warming underneath.

About this piece

This page recreates the choreography of Bloomberg's 2015 feature What's Really Warming the World? on scrolly: one sticky chart, six factors added one step at a time, each dimming into the background once a later factor takes the spotlight. The only author JavaScript on this page is the single Scrolly.init() call at the bottom — every reveal, dim, and color change above is data-show and [data-active-step] CSS.
